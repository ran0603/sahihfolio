# Sahihfolio Brand Book

**Version:** 2.0  
**Date:** October 14, 2026  
**Purpose:** Define how Sahihfolio looks, sounds, and behaves as a trustworthy global halal-investing research product.

Sahihfolio should feel **calm, credible, modern, and useful**—not like a hype-driven trading app, a generic Islamic-finance brochure, or an impersonal fintech dashboard.

## Brand foundation

### Brand idea

**Sahihfolio helps Muslims make informed halal-investing decisions with clarity.**

The name combines:

- **Sahih**: sound, authentic, reliable.
- **Folio**: portfolio, record, personal financial journey.

The brand promise is not “we tell you what is halal.” It is:

> “We help you understand your options, verify what matters, and invest with greater clarity.”

### Positioning

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Category | Halal-investing research and decision-support tool |
| Primary audience | Muslim retail investors researching global investing options |
| Core benefit | Clear, sourced, country-aware comparisons and ongoing portfolio clarity |
| Personality | Knowledgeable, transparent, calm, practical |
| Difference | Practical decision support, not just a ticker-by-ticker compliance verdict |
| Promise | Useful information, clearly sourced, regularly reviewed |

### Brand values

- **Clarity over complexity:** Explain financial and Shariah concepts in plain language.
- **Evidence over opinion:** Show sources, dates, methodologies, and limitations.
- **Respect over pressure:** No fear-based marketing, hype, or unrealistic return promises.
- **Practicality over abstraction:** Help users act on the next useful step.
- **Humility over certainty:** Distinguish research from religious rulings and personalized advice.

## Audience

### Primary persona: The thoughtful beginner

A Muslim professional or student who wants to invest but feels blocked by questions such as:

- “Which platforms are available in my country?”
- “How do I compare fees and account types?”
- “What does this screening methodology actually check?”
- “How do I avoid making a decision based on marketing claims?”

They value practical guidance, transparent sourcing, and a calm interface.

### Secondary personas

| Persona | Need | Sahihfolio’s role |
|---|---|---|
| Self-directed investor | Compare brokers, ETFs, and screening tools | Structured research and comparison |
| Long-term saver | Understand recurring-investment options and costs | Clear fee and suitability context |
| Experienced investor | Track holdings and compliance changes over time | Organized portfolio research workflow |
| Islamic-finance educator | Share reliable explanations with an audience | Citable, well-sourced educational material |

## Voice and tone

### Voice principles

Sahihfolio speaks like a knowledgeable, respectful friend who has done the research—not like a salesperson, scholar issuing fatwas, or a trading influencer.

| Principle | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Clear | “This platform charges a 0.5% currency-conversion fee.” | “Ultra-low-cost global access!” |
| Honest | “We could not verify current availability for this country.” | Hiding uncertainty |
| Respectful | “Screening methodologies can differ; review the basis before deciding.” | “This stock is definitely halal.” |
| Practical | “Compare trading fees, FX fees, and withdrawal costs.” | Generic financial jargon |
| Calm | “Here are the options and trade-offs.” | “Don’t miss out!” |

### Tone by context

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Product UI | Short, direct, reassuring |
| Educational content | Clear, structured, source-led |
| Marketing | Helpful and specific, never hype-driven |
| Error messages | Plain, non-blaming, action-oriented |
| Social media | Useful, respectful, conversational |
| Compliance disclosures | Formal, unambiguous |

### Writing rules

- Use “you” and “we.”
- Prefer short sentences and concrete nouns.
- Explain a term the first time it appears.
- Use “Shariah” consistently in product and educational content.
- Avoid claiming a product is “100% halal” unless the provider explicitly and verifiably makes that claim.
- Avoid “guaranteed,” “risk-free,” “passive income,” “get rich,” and similar pressure language.
- Always distinguish between **information**, **research**, and **advice**.

### Example copy

**Landing headline**

> Find halal-investing options available in your country.

**Subheadline**

> Compare brokers, ETFs, and managed services with clear fees, coverage, and sourcing.

**Empty state**

> Select your country to see relevant options.

**No-results state**

> We do not currently have verified options for this combination. We review new providers regularly.

**Disclaimer**

> Sahihfolio provides general educational information. It does not provide personalized investment, tax, legal, accounting, or Shariah advice. Verify details directly with each provider before making a decision.

## Visual identity

### Design direction

Sahihfolio’s visual identity is a **navy research terminal with emerald intelligence**. It should feel professional and research-oriented in light mode, while offering a serious, focused market-terminal experience in dark mode.

Think:

- Clean institutional financial research tool.
- Calm, dense-but-readable data tables.
- Generous whitespace around long-form content.
- Strong typographic hierarchy.
- One confident emerald action accent.
- Restrained teal for data visualization.
- No crypto-style neon, rainbow charts, heavy gradients, or aggressive urgency.

### Logo concept

Use a simple wordmark:

**Sahihfolio**

Optional monogram:

**Sf** or **S**

Design guidance:

- Use a geometric, modern sans-serif wordmark.
- Keep the logo legible at 24px height.
- Avoid crescents, minarets, gold coins, or clichéd Islamic ornamentation unless used carefully in cultural campaign contexts.
- Avoid stock-market arrows, bull imagery, and trading-app visual clichés.
- The logo should work in dark and light modes.

### Logo usage

| Rule | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Clear space | Leave space equal to the height of the “S” on all sides |
| Minimum size | 120px wide for the full wordmark |
| Backgrounds | Use on plain, high-contrast backgrounds |
| Color | Use Deep Navy, Ink Navy, or white depending on background |
| Modifications | Do not stretch, outline, add shadows, or recolor arbitrarily |

## Color palette

The palette separates **brand**, **interface**, **data**, and **financial meaning**. Navy and slate establish trust; emerald is the primary action accent; teal supports charts and portfolio intelligence; gold is reserved for premium value; green and red are strictly reserved for financial outcomes.

### Semantic financial colors

Do **not** use green or red decoratively. Reserve them for financial meaning so users can scan a screener quickly. Pair every color with an icon, arrow, or text label; color alone is not accessible or reliable for all users.

| Meaning | Light mode | Dark mode | Label / icon pattern |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Positive return / buy signal | `#039855` | `#34D399` | Up arrow + “+4.2%” |
| Negative return / risk flag | `#D92D20` | `#F87171` | Down arrow + “-2.8%” |
| Warning / elevated risk | `#DC6803` | `#FDB022` | Alert icon + “High volatility” |
| Neutral / no signal | `#667085` | `#98A2B3` | Dash + “Insufficient data” |

### Color usage rules

- Use **Deep Navy** for trust-bearing chrome: navigation, headers, and primary brand moments.
- Use **Emerald** as the single clear accent for primary actions and selection—not for every highlight.
- Use **Teal** primarily in charts and data visualization, not as a competing button color.
- Use **Gold** only for premium identity: “Pro screener,” “AI conviction score,” or advanced backtesting.
- Use **green** only for positive financial outcomes or affirmative screening signals.
- Use **red** only for losses, negative changes, or risk flags—never for marketing emphasis.
- Use **amber/orange** for caution, stale data, elevated volatility, or “verify before acting” notices.
- Keep secondary chart series muted: slate blue, teal, gray. Highlight only the metric currently being evaluated.
- Maintain WCAG 2.2 AA contrast for text, icons, and interactive elements.

## Typography

### Typeface direction

Use one modern, highly legible sans-serif family.

Recommended options:

- **Inter**: neutral, technical, excellent for product UI.
- **IBM Plex Sans**: slightly more editorial and institutional.
- **Source Sans 3**: approachable and readable.

For Arabic content, use a high-quality Arabic companion typeface such as **IBM Plex Sans Arabic** or **Noto Sans Arabic**.

### Type scale

| Style | Size / line height | Weight | Use |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Display | 40–56px / 1.1 | 700 | Landing headlines |
| H1 | 32–40px / 1.2 | 700 | Page titles |
| H2 | 24–28px / 1.3 | 600 | Section titles |
| H3 | 18–20px / 1.4 | 600 | Card and sub-section titles |
| Body | 16–18px / 1.6 | 400 | Main content |
| Small | 14px / 1.5 | 400 | Metadata, captions |
| Label | 12–13px / 1.4 | 600 | Form labels, badges |

### Typography rules

- Use sentence case for headings and buttons.
- Avoid all-caps except for small labels or badges.
- Keep paragraphs under roughly 70 characters per line.
- Use tabular numbers for fees, percentages, returns, and financial data.
- Ensure Arabic text has adequate line height and does not force Latin-style letter spacing.

## Layout and UI

### Grid

- Desktop: 12-column grid, maximum content width 1,120px.
- Tablet: 8-column grid.
- Mobile: single-column layout with 16–20px side padding.

### Spacing

Use a 4px base scale:

```text
4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96
```

### Components

| Component | Light mode | Dark mode |
|---|---|---|
| Primary button | Deep Navy `#0B1F3A` or Emerald `#10B981`, depending on hierarchy | Emerald `#34D399` with dark text |
| Secondary button | Slate Blue `#2D4B73` outline or subtle fill | Slate-blue outline on panel surface |
| Cards | White `#FFFFFF`, 1px Cool Gray border, subtle shadow, 12–16px radius | Panel `#161B26`, 1px Slate Border |
| Tables | White surface, Cool Gray row dividers, sticky header | Panel surface, Slate Border dividers, elevated sticky header |
| Forms | White inputs, Cool Gray borders, visible emerald focus state | Panel inputs, Slate Border, visible emerald focus state |
| Badges | Small, rounded, semantic: Verified, Updated, Needs review | Same semantics with dark-mode-safe colors |
| Alerts | Amber for caution; red for errors; green only for confirmed success | Amber, red, and green bright variants |
| Source links | Slate Blue, underlined on hover, external-link icon | Teal or light blue, underlined on hover |

### Data presentation

Financial data should be:

- Aligned consistently, preferably right-aligned for numbers.
- Displayed with currency and units.
- Sourced and timestamped.
- Easy to compare across rows and columns.
- Color-coded only when the color carries financial meaning.

Never display a compliance result without context such as methodology, source, and review date.

## Iconography and imagery

### Icons

- Use a single consistent icon set, such as Lucide or Phosphor.
- Use 1.5px stroke weight.
- Prefer outline icons in product UI.
- Use icons to aid scanning, especially alongside financial status colors.
- Use up/down arrows with percentage changes; use alert icons with risk warnings.

### Photography

Use restrained, real-world imagery:

- People researching calmly on laptops or phones.
- Diverse Muslim professionals and families.
- Everyday financial-life settings.
- Neutral, natural light.

Avoid:

- Stock photos of gold bars, luxury cars, or private jets.
- Generic mosque imagery used as decoration.
- Trading-floor stress imagery.
- Images implying guaranteed wealth.

### Illustration

Use simple, geometric illustrations only when they clarify a concept—for example, comparing fees or explaining a screening workflow. Keep them minimal, using Deep Navy, Slate Blue, Emerald, Teal, and neutral surfaces.

## Product experience principles

### 1. Show the evidence

Every material claim should be traceable.

Each provider or data point should show:

- Source.
- Last-reviewed date.
- Methodology where relevant.
- Any limitation or uncertainty.

### 2. Make trade-offs visible

Do not pretend there is one perfect option. Show fees, coverage, restrictions, and trade-offs side by side.

### 3. Separate research from rulings

Use clear labels:

- “Shariah-screening information”
- “Provider-stated methodology”
- “Sahihfolio editorial note”
- “Not a religious ruling”

### 4. Respect user attention

No dark patterns, fake countdowns, misleading urgency, or hidden fees.

### 5. Design for the next step

Every screen should make the next useful action obvious:

- Compare options.
- Save a result.
- Read the methodology.
- Verify with the provider.
- Join updates.

## Content standards

### Terminology

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Shariah screening | Islamic filter |
| Screening methodology | Halal algorithm |
| Provider | Vendor / app |
| Available in your country | Worldwide |
| Informational result | Guaranteed match |
| Last reviewed | Updated sometime |
| Verify with provider | Trust us |

### Source formatting

Use this pattern:

> Source: Provider pricing page, reviewed 1 October 2026.

For methodology references:

> Methodology: AAOIFI-aligned screening, as described by the provider. This is informational and not a personal religious ruling.

### Claims policy

Before publishing a claim, confirm:

1. The source is primary or authoritative.
2. The claim is current.
3. The wording does not overstate certainty.
4. The review date is recorded.
5. The claim is distinguishable from editorial opinion.

| Channel | Approach |
|---|---|
| SEO / AEO | Answer specific questions clearly, with sources and update dates |
| Email | Practical research updates, not daily hype |
| Social | Short explanations, comparisons, and checklists |
| Partnerships | Work with educators and communities that value transparency |
| Paid ads | Specific, calm value propositions; no get-rich promises |

### Do not say

- “Guaranteed halal returns.”
- “The best halal stocks.”
- “Invest like a pro.”
- “Don’t miss this opportunity.”
- “We handle your Shariah compliance.”
- “This is financial advice.”

## Accessibility and inclusion

- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA for color contrast, focus states, and keyboard navigation.
- Support right-to-left layout for Arabic.
- Use plain English; avoid unexplained financial jargon.
- Respect religious diversity within Muslim communities; avoid presenting one methodology as universally definitive.
- Make disclaimers visible without being obstructive.
- Avoid imagery or copy that assumes every user has the same income, country, family structure, or investment goal.
- Never rely on green/red alone to communicate gain, loss, or risk.

## Brand do’s and don’ts

### Do

- Be specific and sourced.
- Use calm, institutional visuals.
- Explain uncertainty.
- Make comparisons easy.
- Respect the user’s intelligence.
- Keep Islamic-finance language accurate.
- Use emerald for action and teal for data insight.

### Don’t

- Use hype, urgency, or wealth-flex imagery.
- Present editorial research as a fatwa.
- Overpromise certainty.
- Use clichéd Islamic or trading visuals.
- Hide fees, limitations, or affiliate relationships.
- Use lime green, rainbow chart palettes, or heavy gradients.
- Make the product feel like a gambling or crypto app.
